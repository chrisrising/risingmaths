What’s new

  • Two fingers to turn. On a board, put two fingers on the ruler, protractor, mirror, tracing paper or a shape and twist — it turns, the way you would turn a real protractor on a desk.
  • A mirror. A rectangle of glass with a glowing green mirror line down its edge. Whatever sits on the far side shows reflected in it, the same size. Stamp the line onto the page as a dotted line, take the mirror away, and let them draw the image themselves.
  • A plain calculator. A four-function desk calculator alongside the scientific models, with memory, percent and tax keys — for the groups who are not using a scientific one yet.

What it does

Every tool here exists for the same reason: maths makes sense when learners can see what's actually going on. These are for showing meaning rather than demonstrating methods – for the moment someone stops copying your steps and starts understanding why the steps work.

  • Measuring that really is measuring. Rulers and protractors drawn true to scale, so a centimetre on the board is a centimetre. Learners watch a length being measured rather than reading a number off a picture of a ruler.
  • Shapes you can take apart. Drag, rotate, cut and unfold them in front of the class. Area stops being a formula to remember and becomes something they just watched happen – and a net is far more convincing when the solid opens out in front of them.
  • The calculator that's in their hands. The fx-83GT CW+ on screen, key for key. Show the press, and why that button does what it does, instead of describing it from the front while thirty people hunt for it.
  • The point of the lesson, always in view. Objectives sit along the bottom of the board and tick off as you go, so nobody has to wonder where this is heading or what they're meant to be taking from it.
  • Work through it together. Write straight onto worksheets, past papers and slides as you go. Save the annotated copy afterwards for whoever was away, or for the learner who wants the working again without the lesson around it.
  • Attention, when you need it. A timer readable from the back row, and a pen that draws over anything on screen – a website, a video, a photo of someone's book under the visualiser.

Download and set up

  1. Click Download ClassTools (ZIP) above. The file will save to your Downloads folder.
  2. Right-click the ZIP file and choose Extract All. Pick a folder you'll remember, such as Documents.
  3. Open the folder you extracted and double-click ClassTools to start the app.
  4. If Windows shows a "Windows protected your PC" warning, click More info, then Run anyway. This happens with apps that aren't widely downloaded yet.
  5. To keep it handy, right-click the ClassTools icon on your taskbar while it's running and choose Pin to taskbar. It will then be one click away every lesson.
